### Kept, but borderline (judge-wording risk, not a brief conflict)

- **Read-back checks naming "the credit card ending NNNN"** (N1, N6, N7, N8). A34 says "read the product back", and A17 sets the product's spoken name as "credit card ending 4821". An agent that says only "your credit card" in the read-back may be marked down by a strict judge.
- **N4 "first offer: the full 2100 on Friday 16 October"**. A judge that counts the A28 today-ask ("Can you make this payment today?") as an "offer" would misread the order. The brief separates the ask (A28) from the attempts (A30).
- **N8 "no persuasion attempt: the agent proposes no date or amount of its own"**. The mandatory A28 today-ask comes before the job-loss statement. Read literally, that ask proposes the full amount for today. In context it is not a persuasion attempt.

### Script hazards (not leaks; flagged, not fixed)

- **N4.** "Flatly refuses the agent's first offer ... Accepts the agent's second offer". The simulated customer might count the today-ask as the first offer. If so, it would accept attempt 1, and the ledger would read p1 2100 on 2026-10-16 instead of the expected 700 on 2026-10-23. D2 raised the same point. "Cannot pay today" in the script probably prevents this, but it is not guaranteed.
- **N8.** A32 says "If they decline, close as no_promise / hardship." If the customer declines support in one turn and names Friday the 23rd only in a later turn, a correct agent may already say goodbye and close. The persona rule "When the caller says goodbye, say 'Thank you, goodbye.'" then ends the call, and the ledger becomes no_promise/hardship instead of the expected promise. The expected ledger therefore depends on the persona naming the date in the same turn as the decline.
