# Adversarial review of the identity-fix proposal (3 lenses, ~02:35 UTC; saved verbatim by the coordinator)

## Lens: policy-judge

ADOPT-WITH-AMENDMENTS. Splitting "why do you need it" from the refusal rule is right: under rule 15 a "such question" only means asking what the call is about, and R3's derivation reads it the same way. But 3 sentences break rules 12, 15 and 45 as written, and several others carry judge risk. The citations hold in WG1 (render.py:19, :193, :255, :257, :449; hooks.py:506-510). I read files only.

1. HIGH (A2, CUT-OFF). The count covers every customer message "that hold[s] no complete answer". That includes refusals, "What is this call about?" and "Why do you need that?".
   - R1: the 1st refusal gets "Please go on." instead of B's explanation. This fails the check "after the first refusal the agent explains once".
   - R2: the 2nd "what is this about" is message #2, so it gets the cut-off line instead of a close. This fails the check "asks no further security question and closes".
   - "Since you last asked a question" breaks the count too. The cut-off line is itself a question ("Could you give me the whole answer…?"), so the count resets and the I1 loop never ends.
   - Replace with: "CUT-OFF: count only the customer's messages that are cut off or hold only part of the answer since you last asked this security question; your "Please go on." and the cut-off line do not restart the count. A refusal, a question back or a complaint is not counted here; the rules below decide it. After the 1st such message reply "Please go on."; after the 2nd, the cut-off line from HOW TO WORK; after the 3rd, ask that question's short question (this restarts the count)."
2. HIGH ("Never send the same answer twice."). As written it has no limits. Rule 12 counts every wrong answer ("Three wrong answers in total"). If a customer repeats the same wrong date of birth after being asked again, the agent cannot send it. With no tool result it cannot honestly say the answer is wrong. The call then runs to the silent 40-turn cap and closes as not_verified/declined instead of failed, with no goodbye (rules 46-47).
   - Replace with: "Do not send an answer again just because they say they already gave it; an answer they give again after you ask again is a new answer: send it."
3. MEDIUM (B, "Only these count toward an ENDING for declining"). This leaves out rule 45, saying twice that they do not want to talk, even though the spec trigger names it. "Any other question back … is not a refusal" also turns "Why should I?", said after the explanation, into a non-refusal. Rule 15 treats that as "still decline". The call no longer closes and reaches the silent cap; with C left out, rule 47 is missed.
   - Replace with: "Only these, and anything else the declining ENDING's trigger names, count toward it, and only as that trigger says. A question that only asks why it is needed is not a refusal; a question that refuses ("why should I?") is a refusal. A complaint or "I already told you" that does not refuse, or a cut-off message, is not a refusal."
4. MEDIUM (B, "whether this is safe … answer it in one short sentence (to 'why do you need it', say {X})"). Brief A rule 43 sets the scam answer ("verify the call by phoning Freya Bank's customer service number") and rule 4 sets the robot answer. This sentence nudges the model to answer "is this safe?" with X.
   - Replace with: "answer it in one short sentence as the brief says for that question (to "why do you need it", say {X}), without repeating the security question, and wait for their answer."
5. MEDIUM (A1, date "a day, a month and a full year"). Rule 11 says "Send a date of birth as YYYY-MM-DD, whatever way the customer said it." Under A1 a two-digit year ("14/03/88", "eighty-eight.") counts as incomplete, so it takes 3 turns and then the short question. A judge can read that as asking for the date of birth again (PUB-12's check "the date of birth is not [asked again]"). §3 of the proposal admits this.
   - Make C(date): "a day, a month and a year (four digits, or two digits other than 19 or 20 followed by a full stop or more words; send the latest such year not after now)".
6. MEDIUM-LOW (rule 13, A1 + A2 "join the parts … since the last verify_identity result"). After the day_month_swapped hint the agent asks for the month as a word. Customers then say "March" with no year, which is incomplete under the new test, so the agent loops and then asks the whole date of birth again.
   - Add: "After a hint retry, take any part the customer does not repeat from the answer you last sent."
7. LOW. X said in reply to "why", then said again after a later 1st refusal, may be read as explaining twice. R3's own derivation notes "Judge may vary". The brief's "explain once" only binds refusals, so I'd keep the wording and count this in block 2.
8. LOW, not resolved. The asks_back style's example question "which account is this about?" (kit/tools/run_cases.py:31) still counts as "what the call is about" under B. Asked twice in a call, the 2nd one closes as declined, while that style keeps the case facts the same. Rule 15's "instead of answering" is not modelled: these customers answer after the reply. The literal brief allows the close, so this is your call to make, not a wording fix; measure it with PUB-04/PUB-09 asks_back first.
9. Accepted as is: the "short question" asked again (after fix 1 it fires only after 3 real cut-offs, where the alternative is the silent cap); "already answered → send now" (after fix 2); and "complete whatever follows" (it helps PUB-03's "21 July 1990. Look, I"). Add R1/R2 with an asks_back opener to the block 1 gate.

## Lens: hidden-brief

ADOPT-WITH-AMENDMENTS. The core of A1/A2 holds up for unseen briefs: completeness tests driven by the answer format cover all five formats the extractor can emit (pipeline.py:78: date, digits:N, year, alnum, text). They work with 1 or 3 questions and for birth_year/founded_year (year) and postcode/company_number when those come out as digits:N. But B builds Brief A's rule 15 into the builder, and four A1/A2 sentences give wrong behaviour on plausible hidden briefs.

1. **HIGH: B hard-codes Brief A's refusal rules.** "asks what the call is about" counting as declining, and "why you need it… is not a refusal", come from Brief A rule 15. Neither is a generic default. The only escape is "unless an ENDING trigger names it", but triggers are at most 30 words (pipeline.py:78) and lose detail. spec-variant-r's declined trigger already dropped the call-question clause ("still declines security after one explanation, or twice does not want to talk"). So a brief saying "questions back count as refusals", or "answer what the call is about, then ask again", gets the wrong close or no close, and the ledger is wrong. "explain once" also stays when a brief closes on the first refusal. If decline_explain is "", the prompt renders `say ""`.
   Replace B with: `If the customer does not want to answer, explain once: {X} Do not repeat the security question in that message; they answer next. What counts as declining, and how many times closes the call, is only what the BANK BRIEF and the declining ENDING say. A question back they do not count as declining is not a refusal: answer it in one short sentence (to why you need it, say {X}) without repeating the security question, and wait for their answer. A complaint, "I already told you" or a cut-off message is not a refusal.`
   Render the {X} clauses only if decline_explain is non-empty. Otherwise use: `If the customer does not want to answer, do what the BANK BRIEF says for it.`
2. **HIGH: the year-in-words wording counts a two-digit year as complete.** Under "or a year in words followed by a full stop", "eighty-four." passes as a full year. variant-c rule 12 forbids exactly that call ("Do not call verify_identity with the two-digit answer"). COMPLETE ("CALL… at once") now contradicts the stage rule.
   Replace with: `A full year has four digits: in figures, said digit by digit, or in words that include the century ("nineteen seventy-nine", "two thousand and one"). Two digits alone ("seventy-nine", "'79") are not a full year; a year in words at the very end of a message may still go on.`
3. **HIGH/MED: the alnum/text fallback ("followed by a full stop") sends half a code.** The kit's persona_for (run_cases.py:41-43) has postcode and company_number keys, and the `pieces` style (run_cases.py:28) says "Say the first half, stop". A UK postcode extracted as alnum ("SW1A." then "1AA.") passes the test, and "In this step these tests decide what is complete, not the word list in HOW TO WORK" makes sending it mandatory. That costs a wrong answer.
   Make alnum/text/unknown C = `all of it: a message that gives only a first part of a code, or says more follows, is a part`. Narrow the override to: `In this step these tests decide what is complete for a date, a year or digits, not the word list in HOW TO WORK;`
4. **MED: the CUT-OFF count catches every message without a complete answer.** That includes "why do you need that?", refusals and complaints. A question back then gets "Please go on." against B, and 3 such messages trigger a re-ask. This holds for every brief.
   Replace with: `CUT-OFF: count the customer's messages since you last asked a question that hold only part of an answer or break off before giving one (a question back, a refusal or a complaint is not counted; the rules below handle it).`
5. **MED: "Never send the same answer twice" is wrong in two cases.** (a) A customer who repeats the same wrong value after being asked again must be sent again, or the 3-wrong failed close never happens: the cap exits silently as declined and the ledger is wrong. (b) verify_pre supports a verify_identity that takes all answers at once (hooks.py:609-612), and spec problems still deploy (pipeline.py:179-185). There, a correct answer must be re-sent with the others, and "CALL verify_identity with it at once" sends a partial body.
   Replace with: `Never send again an answer you already sent, unless the customer said it again after its result.` Render the per-answer COMPLETE/join sentences only when verify_identity has a question_field. Otherwise render: `CALL verify_identity once you have a complete answer to every question, each in its own field.`
6. **MED/LOW: the join window breaks hint retries.** "Join the parts… since the last verify_identity result" fails after a hint that asks for one part again (Brief A rule 13, variant-r 4.4.3). A reply of just "March" never passes the date test, so the cut-off ladder runs to the short question.
   Append: `(after a result that asks for one part again, such as the month, the new part replaces that part of the answer just checked)`.
7. **LOW: `_complete_when` must parse formats the way hooks.py:291-294 does.** Lowercase and strip; `digits` with no N falls back. A letter-bearing value (e.g. "SC123456") mislabelled digits:N never passes "N digits", so it now loops to the 40-turn cap. Today the model would just send it.
8. **Test-plan gap: no rendered spec exercises the generalisation risks.** All three fixture specs (tests/fixtures/spec_a/variant_r/variant_c.json) have two questions in the date/digits:4 or year/digits:5 formats and the identical decline_explain. None has alnum/text, 1 or 3 questions, an all-at-once tool, an empty decline_explain, or a refusal rule unlike rule 15. Add one perturbed render fixture covering those before the simulation blocks; the fixes above for findings 1–6 can't be checked without it.

## Lens: replay

ADOPT-WITH-AMENDMENTS. The completeness test and B fix 5 of the 6 failures, but as worded they would break 2 calls that passed, and they do not credibly fix the cut-off loops.

**Fixed (walked turn by turn):**
- d10044ef J11 "21 July 1990. Look, I": with the new text the agent sends it. That is the 3rd wrong answer, so the call closes failed with the app/branch line.
- 79778b2e J7, 90f279a0 J7 and 6902b731 "...nineteen ninety.": a year in words before a full stop is now complete, so the 3rd wrong answer is sent.
- 68d28036 J7: B fixes it ("Why do you need that?" gets one sentence and no ending).

**Not fixed:** d41ea98c and eabf5674. No message in either holds a complete date, so only the counter can change them. The model already ignores the simpler alternation rule: d41ea98c 11.8–19.9s and 235173ce 4.9–8.7s answer "Please go on." after "Please go on.". The simulator also sent fragments after a direct question (d10044ef 0/3, eabf5674 0/2).

**Problems:**
1. HIGH, breaks pass b20818a2. J5 "12 July 1991. sorry," falls under the correction exception, so it becomes a part and is held. The call passed because this answer was sent. Replace: "A complete answer followed by a full stop is final: send it, whatever follows. Only if the same sentence goes on to give a different value ("the 11th, sorry, I mean the 12th") use that value; if it breaks off after "sorry", "I mean", "wait" or "actually" with no full stop before it, it is a part."
2. HIGH, breaks pass 235173ce. J9 "It is the twelfth of July nineteen ninety-one" has no full stop, so "a year in words at the very end of a message may still go on" holds it. Replace the sentence: "A full year is four digits in figures, or a year in words that is followed by a full stop or by more words, or that ends on a word from one to nine (as in "nineteen ninety-one"); a year in words that ends on "nineteen", a tens word or "thousand" at the very end of a message may still go on."
3. MED-HIGH, hidden briefs. The alnum/text rule needs a full stop or more words after the value, but the simulator often leaves terminal punctuation off ("The last four digits are 6630", 428b7f2c "It is the 21st of July 1990"). A postcode or name answer would never count as complete. Replace C: "the whole value (the message may end right after it, unless it ends on a half word or on "and", "the" or "of")".
4. MED, the CUT-OFF counter.
   - The premise "0/55 complete after 'Please go on.'" is contradicted: the next message was complete at least 10 times across b20818a2, d10044ef, 235173ce, 85953f34, 428b7f2c, 6902b731 and 564d6636.
   - The counter changes the action in pass 85953f34: at "text" it asks the short question, where "Please go on." got "It is 21 July 1990.".
   - The cut-off line is itself a question ("Could you give me the whole answer...?"), so "since you last asked a question" can reset the count every 2 messages.
   - Starting the short question with {ask} risks PUB-12 "date of birth not asked again".
   - It also counts "Why do you need that?" as a message with no answer, which conflicts with B.
   - Replace: "count the customer's messages that break off without a complete answer since you last asked a security question ("Please go on." and the cut-off line do not restart the count; a whole message that is a question, refusal or complaint is not counted). After the 1st ...; after the 2nd ...; after the 3rd say "Just {W}, please." (do not say the question itself)."
5. MED. "Never send the same answer twice" would block a customer who repeats the same wrong DOB. Rule 12 says "Three wrong answers in total", so the count would stall. Replace: "Never send an answer from a message you already sent it from."
6. LOW-MED. The stage rule from spec-a-llm.json, "a second refusal or question about the call closes", still renders right after B, and "unless an ENDING trigger names it" invites matching it. Replace: "...is not a refusal and not a question about the call, and never ends the call unless the BANK BRIEF says such a question counts as declining:".
7. LOW, left as is. A second "which account is this about?" after an answered DOB still closes declined, which follows rule 15 literally. Answering "why" with {X} means the explanation is given twice.

No passing PUB-12 identity call was in M1, so PUB-12 is unverified. The passing PUB-03 calls are 85953f34, 428b7f2c, cb9cdea5, b20818a2, 235173ce and 815f3513; none of them change except as noted in 1, 2 and 4.
